# Leek and Mushroom Quiche

**Contributor:** Frances Biral

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Director's Office

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 6-8 6 large mushrooms, trimmed 1 large leek 3 tablespoons butter 1 tablespoon oil 1 tablespoon all-purpose flour 6 ozs. Swiss cheese 2 tablespoons fresh parsley 4 large extra large eggs, beaten 1 1/3 cups half-and-half 1 teaspoon (or less) salt freshly ground pepper 1 teaspoon dried oregano frozen deep-dish pie shell

---

## Instructions

Preheat oven to 375Â° F. Slice mushrooms and leeks. Shred cheese. Chop parsley. (Slicing, shredding and chopping can be done with the food processor.) Heat butter and oil in a skillet and sautÃ© leek over moderately high heat until softened. Stir in flour and warm it, but do not let it cook. Remove skillet from heat. Stir sliced mushrooms into leek mixture. Put ingredients into a large bowl. Add beaten eggs, half-and-half, cheese, salt and pepper. Pour mixture into the frozen pie shell. Sprinkle top with oregano and parsley and bake in a 375Â° oven for 40 minutes, or until top is well colored. Let stand for 15 minutes before serving. It can also be served at room temperature.

## About This Recipe

This dish is a good choice for potluck parties because it can be served at room temperature or quickly reheated in the host's oven. It was popular at the Museum Administration Department's annual potluck Christmas lunch held at the Villa. Usually an attempt was made to coincide the lunch with John Walsh's birthday, making it even more fun. In December 1995 a digitally doctored well-known photo of Richard Nixon and Elvis Presley with John's head over Nixon's body was created and his birthday card continued the Elvis theme, with one staff member writing "hunka hunka burning director." She had just joined the department and was quite brave to write those words! The quiche can also be made using low fat ingredients, without sacrificing its taste, by substituting Eggbeaters for the eggs, undiluted evaporated skim milk for the half-and-half, oil for the butter, and using a lower fat Swiss cheese.
