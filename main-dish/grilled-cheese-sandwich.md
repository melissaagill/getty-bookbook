# Grilled Cheese Sandwich

**Contributor:** Chris Lakey

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Manuscripts Curatorial

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 1 sourdough bread cheddar cheese (preferably aged for 5 or more years) Swiss or Monterey jack or Provolone cheese (any white cheese really) butter, only butter patience TLC (that is to say, tender loving care)

---

## Instructions

Place large nonstick skillet on burner. Butter one side of the first piece of bread; lay that piece (buttered side down) on the skillet. When buttering your bread, it is important not to drown the bread with the butter; simply spread a thin layer of butter over both slices of bread. If you put too much butter on the bread, the sandwich will taste like butter. Cut the cheese; place on bread in skillet. It is best to alternate white/cheddar/white/cheddar so you will have the maximum mix of cheese in every bite. Butter the remaining slice of bread; place unbuttered side on the cheese side of the bread already in the skillet. Light the stove; place heat on low-medium. Wait. For the first five minutes, alternate cooking each side of the sandwich, flipping every minute or so. After the initial grilling time, allow each side of the sandwich to grill for approximately 3 minutes each. Grill the sandwich on both sides twice. At this point, the bread should look rather brown on both sides; the cheese should be melted to the point of escaping onto the skillet. This is a perfect grilled cheese. Put sandwich on a plate. Garnish with ranch dressing for dipping purposes. 15 minutes is the minimum; the best grilled cheese sandwiches are those which are meticulously cared for on the stove. If you cook it too quickly, it will plain suck. If you burn it, you are an idiot. The most important thing to remember about making a grilled cheese sandwich is not to rush the melting process by "turning up the heat." This is always a bad idea and will undoubtedly burn your bread. All Rights Reserved, Holden Inc.

## About This Recipe

Chris Lakey lives in Los Angeles and is sick and tired of eating grilled cheese sandwiches that are cooked improperly. This recipe has been in Mr. Lakey's family since the 18th century, when his great-great-grandfather was the King of Denmark. Grilled cheese sandwiches can be eaten at any point during the day and night. There is not a bad time to eat a grilled cheese sandwich. Cheese may vary by regions. Under no circumstances should meat products come into contact with the grilled cheese; there is simply no reason for such an interaction. If you put anything in a grilled cheese sandwich besides cheese, it is no longer a grilled cheese but some weird knock-off.
