# Black Beans - Caribbean style

**Contributor:** Carol Donovan

**Getty Entity:** Trust

**Department:** Information Technology Services, Client Services

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 8 1 lb. black beans, soaked overnight in cold water 1/3 cup olive or canola oil 1 medium-sized onion 2 scallions, white bulbs and green stalks, minced (or 1 leak) 4 cloves garlic, minced 1/2 green bell pepper, seeded and diced 1/2 red bell pepper, seeded and diced (orange or yellow are a nice substitution) 2 tablespoons tomato paste 3 tablespoons red wine vinegar 1 tablespoon dried oregano 1 tablespoon ground cumin 3 tablespoons fresh coriander (cilantro), minced 1 tablespoon salt 1 teaspoon pepper The original recipe calls for a minced jalapeÃ±o pepper, but this makes it extremely spicy.

---

## Instructions

Drain beans. Place in a large stockpot with water to cover. Heat to boil; lower heat and simmer 1 1/4 hours. Add water to keep moist. Now the fun part comes that is making the sofrito - spices and things that make this dish interesting. Heat oil in medium-sized skillet. Add onion, scallions, garlic, peppers, and sautÃ©. Stir frequently for 7 minutes. Stir in tomato paste and vinegar and add oregano, cumin, coriander/cilantro, salt and pepper. Reduce heat and cook 5 minutes. Remove from heat. Add sofrito to cooked beans (there should be a fair amount of liquid with the beans). Simmer uncovered until thick, approximately 30 minutes. Serve or refrigerate and reheat.

## About This Recipe

About fifteen years ago Carol worked for a caterer (in between regular jobs). This was her favorite vegetarian dish that was served, and she used to make these black beans for potluck type functions. A friend asked her for the recipe because her husband raved about the beans and it is a good thing Carol gave it to her, because later she lost it. Last year when they got together, the woman who had the recipe thanked her again for them and told her they remain her husband's favorite beans. Carol now asked her for the recipe. Today it is in soft copy, too.
