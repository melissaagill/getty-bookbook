# Sicilian Eggplant with Mint and Garlic

**Contributor:** Anna Zagorski

**Getty Entity:** Conservation Institute

**Department:** nan

---

## Servings & Ingredients

small Japanese eggplants (not longer than 5 inches) salt fresh mint fresh garlic olive oil canned peeled Roma tomatoes or fresh tomatoes peeled garlic oregano capers olive oil onion

---

## Instructions

Peel the eggplant but do not remove the stem. If the eggplant is very fresh - just recently picked - then you can skip the next step. Salt eggplant and leave in colander to drain bitter juice for at least 1/2 hour, then rinse and pat dry. Make 3 or 4 small incisions in eggplant and insert a sliver of garlic and some mint leaves. In a heavy skillet heat up a generous amount of olive oil; once it is very hot fry eggplant a couple of minutes on each side until they are a nice golden color. It is very important that the olive oil be quite hot - this way the eggplant doesn't soak up the oil and is a lovely golden on the outside while the interior is deliciously creamy and not oily. This is crucial. Once the eggplant is a lovely golden color place them on a plate with a paper towel and blot any excess oil. Sprinkle with a bit of salt. Let them cool to room temperature and they are ready to eat as an appetizer arranged on a plate with some lemon wedges and olives or you can make a sauce to accompany them which you can serve over your favorite pasta. Sauce: Fry onions in olive oil until they are soft and translucent. Add in one crushed garlic clove and continue to sautÃ© everything together for another 2 minutes. Add a sprig of oregano, being careful not to overdo it as oregano is a very strong flavor. Add canned or fresh tomatoes; it is a good idea to chop them beforehand so they will cook faster. Let everything simmer together for at least 20 minutes; once the sauce starts to thicken add some capers (again this is to your taste as they have a strong flavor as well and can overpower the sauce. You will need to experiment a bit). Carefully place the fried eggplant into the simmering sauce and continue to simmer for another 15 minutes. Season with salt and pepper to taste. The sauce is now ready to serve over pasta. Garnish each dish with one eggplant and a sprig of mint, or cool the sauce to room temperature and serve the eggplant as an appetizer with a dollop of the sauce on the side, garnished with lemon wedges and olives.

## About This Recipe

Anna's favorite pastime involves good friends over a long and leisurely meal of good food. As far as she is concerned, the eggplant is king of the vegetable world, but it is tricky to cook. If not made correctly, its taste may bring to mind a sponge soaked in oil, and that is why a lot of people have been turned off by this purple beauty (the white ones are fabulous as well). Anna has been collecting eggplant recipes from around the world for the last few years and is especially inspired by the cuisine of Turkey where the eggplant is truly appreciated. This year she is pleased to say the eggplants in her garden are stunning; all owed to compost and organic snail bait.
