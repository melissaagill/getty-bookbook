# Primo Pesto Sauce

**Contributor:** Tulis McCall

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Manuscripts Curatorial

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves variable. It depends on your guests: reserved eaters or bottomless pits? Recommended doses: 2 cups fresh basil leaves packed firmly (For an adventure try Siam Queen or African Blue. No Cinnamon Basil.) 4 large garlic cloves 1/2 cup pignoli (pinenuts), toasted in a dry, preferably cast iron frying pan over low heat; stir frequently to avoid turning them completely black making them revolting 1/4 - 1/2 cup olive oil 1/2 cup Parmesan cheese, grated (as they say in Michigan "parmeeeshun") 1/4 cup Romano cheese, grated 1/4 cup lemon juice, freshly squeezed 1/8 cup parsley salt and pepper The main ingredients you will play around with are the olive oil, lemon juice, and salt and pepper.

---

## Instructions

Put basil, garlic and pine nuts in blender in stages. Add just enough olive oil to get it moving about. Add a bit of the lemon juice to lubricate. Add all the cheese in stages. Keep alternating the olive oil and lemon juice until you achieve a pleasing consistency as well as balance. It is not necessary to use all the oil or lemon juice. Add a sprig or so of parsley into the blending blender. Start sparingly as the parsley can easily overwhelm everything else.  Add salt and pepper as you think best. Taste to see if you have achieved a sort of quintessential piquancy which will be peculiar to your chef's palate and yet not startle your guests. Let stand for about an hour and taste again. If it doesn't suit you, mess with it until it does. Then mangia.
