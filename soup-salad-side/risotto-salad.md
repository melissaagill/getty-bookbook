# Risotto Salad

**Contributor:** Diane Mooradian

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Paintings Conservation

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 6-8 2 6-oz. jars (or 1 14-oz. jar) Cara Mia marinated artichoke hearts 1 6 or 7-oz. package chicken flavored rice mix 2 scallions, thinly sliced 1/2 green or red pepper, seeded and chopped small can of green or black sliced olives 1/4 teaspoon curry powder 1/3 cup mayonnaise

---

## Instructions

Drain artichoke hearts, reserving marinade. Cook rice as package directs, omitting the butter. Remove from heat, turn into a bowl, and let stand until cool. Add onions, green or red pepper, olives and artichoke hearts. Combine artichoke marinade with curry powder and mayonnaise; mix lightly. Add to rice mixture. Cover and refrigerate until well chilled.


---

## About This Recipe

This recipe is from a well-used cookbook entitled "The Heart of Cooking with Cara Mia," put out by our friends in Castroville, the Artichoke capitol of the world. For the last eighteen years, Diane has been bringing this dish (doubled) to various Getty potlucks where it is always well received. (She can cook other dishes, honest!) Present it in an attractive serving bowl, garnished with lettuce and tomato wedges if you want to get fancy. It travels well and is quite delicious.

## Getty Memories

This risotto recipe harkens back to the many potlucks we shared at lunchtimes on the sun-dappled Ranch House porch at the Getty Villa, beginning in the early 1980s to celebrate various happy occasions. Food has always been an important part of life at the Getty, going back to the "cooking classes" held in the kitchen off the old Founder's Room on Saturdays, sometimes led by visiting scholars such as Sir Francis Watson. The Villa Christmas parties in those years were quite splendiferous, with elegant dishes and such desserts as cream puffs in the shape of swans. Even though I just missed the infamous toga parties of the late 70s, the Villa Closing Party in July of 1997 starring John Walsh as Neptune has to rank up there with the best of them.....but for me the best Getty party of all was my own wedding reception on the Ranch House lawn in June 1991.
