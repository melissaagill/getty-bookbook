# Tom Yam Kung (Sour and Spicy Thai Soup)

**Contributor:** Kristin Kelly

**Getty Entity:** Conservation Institute

**Department:** Director's Office

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 4-6 15-18 shrimp, shelled and deveined but with the tails still on 12-15 mushrooms, cut in quarters (in Thailand, these would be angel or straw mushrooms, but white mushrooms work as well) 2 lemon grass stalks, cut into 1" lengths 5 slices galangal, peeled (ginger can be substituted) 3 shallots, sliced 4 kaffir lime leaves, torn (kaffir lime leaves are available at the Thai markets on Hollywood Boulevard, and also in the markets in Little Saigon in Westminster; they freeze well; it is also easy to grow kaffir lime trees in pots in Southern California) 3-4 small hot chiles, just broken in a mortar and pestle 4 tablespoon lime juice 3-4 tablespoon fish sauce (this is pretty potent stuff for the uninitiated so watch it; it is available as above, and also in many supermarkets) 4 sprigs fresh cilantro 4 cups water

---

## Instructions

In a large pot heat water to boiling and remove from heat. Add the lemon grass, galangal, shallots, kaffir lime leaves, chile, lime juice, and fish sauce. Adjust the balance as desired. With a slotted spoon, remove the lemon grass, shallots, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, and chile from the broth. (This is very un-Thai, but it does make it a lot easier to eat. Make sure that you get all the chile seeds out or your mouth will be on fire for a few hours!) Add the shrimp and the mushrooms, and adjust the seasoning again. Serve hot with a sprig of cilantro on top of each bowl.


---

## About This Recipe

Thai cooking is a balance of flavors--salty (the fish sauce), hot (chiles), sour (the two limes), and, in many recipes (although not this one), sweet. It is imperative with this soup not to allow the chiles to take over. The salty and sour flavors are just as important.

## Getty Memories

Thailand is a place of contrasts. In the megalopolis of Bangkok (whose abbreviated Thai name, Krung Thep, means "City of Angels"), brightly colored temples and traditional wooden buildings are buried in a modern onslaught of huge skyscrapers and almost incomprehensible traffic. Children here, it is said, lose seven IQ points by the time they are ten due to excessive lead from auto emissions in the air. Sunrises are supernaturally bright, and the canals, or klongs, are frighteningly polluted. The city seems almost uninhabitable, yet it is home to countless millions of Thais and immigrants from all over Southeast Asia. Under the noise, chaos, and pollution found here lies one of the most vibrant of Asian cultures, one infused with the gentle principles of Theravada Buddhism and a languid tropical approach to life. Even after years of visiting the country, I find it simultaneously incomprehensible and the most exciting place I have ever been. I learned to make this soup at a cooking class in Bangkok. Preparing it infuses my house with the sharp smells of the country. Make it, close your eyes, and breathe deeply. You will instantly be transported to the Land of Smiles!
