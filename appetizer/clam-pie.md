# Clam Pie

**Contributor:** Jessica Berman

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Preparation

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 12 3 cans (approx. 6 ounces each) minced clams 1 teaspoon lemon juice 1 medium-sized onion 1 small green pepper 1/4 cup parsley 2-3 cloves garlic (or more, to taste) 1 teaspoon oregano 1 stick unsalted butter (1/4 lb.) 1 cup plain breadcrumbs Parmesan cheese, grated crackers (Ritz or something similar)

---

## Instructions

In a blender or food processor combine the onion, green pepper, parsley, garlic and oregano. Mix until it becomes a well-blended green mush and set aside. Place clams (including their juice) in a sautÃ© pan, add lemon juice, and simmer for about 10 minutes. While the clams are simmering, melt the butter in a separate sautÃ© pan, add the blended green mixture and sautÃ© for several minutes. Place the breadcrumbs in a large mixing bowl and add the clams and liquid. Mix well (it should end up a pasty, thick mix). Add the sautÃ©ed green mush to the bowl and mix until well-blended. It should be a thick mixture in the end; if it seems runny (like a cake batter), add more breadcrumbs, a little at a time. Empty the mixture into an ungreased pie plate. Sprinkle Parmesan cheese over the entire surface. (At this point, you can freeze the clam pie for a later date, bake right away, or refrigerate it, baking it later the same day.) Bake uncovered at 350Â° F until bubbly and slightly brown on top, about 15-30 minutes if fresh; 30-60 minutes if frozen. Goes well with Ritz crackers, but any cracker will do.

## About This Recipe

Jessica states: "Trust me, you do not need to be a clam lover to love clam pie." This family recipe, which has been a coffee table staple at Thanksgivings in New Jersey for as long as she can remember, has won converts on both coasts. Her Long Island cousins rush in the door, toss their coats on the ground and fight for space around the pie plate; her husband routinely scans the calendar, searching for the next holiday, which could mean there is a Clam Pie in his future. However, there need be no event to legitimize the making of a Clam Pie. "It's Sunday and I got out of bed" will do just fine.
