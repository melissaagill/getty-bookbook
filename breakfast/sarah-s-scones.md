# Sarah's Scones

**Contributor:** Lisa Vihos

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Education

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Makes about 20 scones 2 cups all-purpose flour 1/3 cup sugar 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder 1/2 teaspoon baking soda 1/4 teaspoon salt 6 tablespoons butter or margarine, softened 1/2 cup buttermilk 1 extra large egg 1 1/2 teaspoons vanilla 2/3 cup dried currants

---

## Instructions

Adjust oven rack to lowest level and preheat oven to 400Â° F. Stir dry ingredients together in large bowl. Cut butter into dry mixture using a pastry blender. In a small bowl, mix together buttermilk, egg, and vanilla. Add to dry ingredients, along with currants. Stir until dough holds together. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured board. If needed, use hands to completely incorporate the ingredients. Gently pat dough into 1/2-inch-thick pancake and cut out circles using a cookie cutter or small glass (flour the rim). Place scones on ungreased baking sheet and bake for 10 minutes or until a light golden (not brown) color.

## About This Recipe

This scone recipe was given to Lisa by her friend Sarah of Los Alamos. When Lisa began to bake scones for departmental gatherings, several people went wild over the recipe and in turn gave it to their friends, and so on. One report has it that upon sampling these scones, an elderly gentleman requested his wife prepare him a batch every day. Darn good and easy to make; they are wonderful to make with young children, who enjoy getting their hands sticky and cutting out the circles.

## Getty Memories

My favorite Getty food event was the costume party that closed the Villa. I remember one food item in particular, not for its flavor, but for its presentation. Some kind of yummy concoction was served on individual spoons. Guests were invited to select a spoon, gulp down the delicacy, and return the spoon to the tray. It was wonderfully decadent. Everyone was dressed up in Roman garb, John as Neptune welcomed us from the porch overlooking the main peristyle garden, and later we all drank champagne and reveled in the fountain. All in all, from the food to the general atmosphere, it was a remarkable event.
