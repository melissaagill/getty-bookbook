# Buttermilk Pancakes

**Contributor:** Janet Grossman

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Antiquities Curatorial

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 3 adult men or 4 adult women or 5 to 6 children 2 cups unbleached all-purpose flour, unsifted (important not to sift) 3 tablespoons sugar 1 teaspoon salt 1 teaspoon soda 2 cups buttermilk 2 extra large eggs 3 to 4 tablespoons vegetable oil

---

## Instructions

Mix the flour, sugar, salt and soda together thoroughly. In a separate bowl, beat the eggs into the buttermilk, then stir in the oil. Pour this liquid mixture into the flour mixture. Stir both the wet and dry ingredients together with a light touch. Do not beat the mixture, but stir with cutting and chopping motions. Your aim is to barely dampen the dry mixture with the wet. Let the batter sit at room temperature about 15 minutes until bubbles appear from the interaction between the buttermilk, salt and soda. You want the pancakes to be light, not heavy and chewy, and to achieve this goal you must let a bit of action take place in the batter! Pancakes are best cooked on a rectangular griddle. The one Janet has used for years is made of heavy aluminum that has a Teflon coating. A griddle will span two burners on most stoves. Put the heat on medium high (gas or electric) and let the griddle get hot, but not smoking. If it is smoking, it is too hot. If you are using a new griddle or one without nonstick coating, season it with light layer of vegetable oil before attempting to cook the pancakes on it. To make sure your griddle is the correct temperature, sprinkle a few drops of water on it--they should sizzle and evaporate immediately. Using a large spoon, put a generous spoonful for each pancake on the griddle. Leave space in between each cake so you have room to flip. Flip each pancake when the batter is just barely set and the first bubbles in the batter have popped. Serve directly from the stove on warmed plates with warm syrup.

## About This Recipe

When Janet Grossman was approaching her thirtieth birthday, she married a man with four children, aged twelve to seventeen. Combined with her 2 1/2 year old son, an instant large family was formed. New situations and change are hard on everyone, but Janet's pancakes managed to calm the worse temper tantrum of the two year old and cure the sullen silence of one of the teenagers. Some days, the pancakes were the only good thing that occurred and saved the day for everyone! Janet and the father of the teenagers are still married 25 years later and she still makes buttermilk pancakes, but especially at family gatherings.
