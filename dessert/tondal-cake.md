# Tondal Cake

**Contributor:** Beth Morrison

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Manuscripts Curatorial

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 8 1 package butter cake mix (not yellow, specifically butter) 1 3 1/2-oz. package vanilla pudding (instant is fine) 1/2 cup rum 1/2 cup oil 1/2 cup water 4 extra large eggs 1/2 cup pecans Glaze: 1 cup sugar 1/2 stick butter 1/4 cup rum 1/4 cup water

---

## Instructions

Preheat oven to 325Â° F. Combine first 6 ingredients and mix well with mixer (it will be very thick); pour into a Bundt pan prepared with shortening or Pam (it is impossible to use too much shortening or spray to make sure the cake turns over well at the end); sprinkle batter with pecans and bake for 55 minutes. Prepare glaze by combining sugar, butter, rum, and water in a small pan (simmer for 5 minutes or until slightly thickened); remove cake from oven and pour glaze over cake (use a knife to gently pry the sides of the cake from the pan and pour glaze down). Place cake back in the oven for 10 minutes or until glaze has slightly hardened. Remove cake from oven and immediately turn upside down onto a plate (the cake will sometimes stick, but it tastes just as delicious whether perfect or not!)

## About This Recipe

The Department of Manuscripts has a truly wonderful manuscript that tells the story in words and pictures of a medieval knight named Tondal who was taken by an angel on an adventurous journey through heaven and the underworld. The manuscript's stunning illuminations graphically evoke the fate that awaits thieves, murderers, and misers until Tondal is finally convinced to live a good and worthwhile life. This delicious (i.e. fattening) pecan-rum cake now bears Tondal's name because while you are eating it, you are convinced you are in heaven, but then again, with all the calories, you know there will be .... to pay.

## Getty Memories

It is likely that anyone who has run into Beth at a small Getty-related function over the last four years has also tasted a specimen of her baking. Departmental birthday parties, open houses, good-bye parties, softball games; whatever the occasion, she likes to have a baked good ready and waiting. For her baking is therapeutic, but more than that, she knows that people really appreciate the time, effort, and especially, the final results. Beth comes from a rather large family, but most of them live very far from here, so her friends and co-workers at the Getty are her Los Angeles family. In this land of palm trees and sunshine, she enjoys sharing some of the old-fashioned Midwestern hospitality she grew up with, and she has found that nothing lights up a room full of Getty people quite as quickly as a plate of cookies, slices of apple pie, or squares of home-made brownies. Beth is convinced that a little bit of time combined with large quantities of butter and sugar builds Getty community faster than anything else, except, of course, really great art!
