# Baked Alaska

**Contributor:** Mitchell Bishop

**Getty Entity:** Conservation Institute

**Department:** Information and Communications

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 4-6 1 pint best available quality vanilla ice cream 1 large ripe pineapple 3 extra large eggs 6 tablespoons sugar 1/4-1/3 cup Bacardi 151 proof rum

---

## Instructions

Select a large, ornamental and ripe pineapple. Slice a side off the pineapple leaving approximately two-thirds of the pineapple remaining. Scoop out contents of pineapple and set aside. Allow vanilla ice cream to soften until it can be easily handled. Mix it with the pineapple cut into small chunks (1/4 to 1/2 inch). Place the mixture back into the pineapple leaving some room for the meringue and then place pineapple in freezer to refreeze the mixture. About half an hour before serving prepare the meringue. Separate whites of eggs and place into a bowl. Beat egg whites until stiff slowly adding sugar. Spoon mixture into pineapple and draw it up to form decorative peaks. Immediately place under a broiler to lightly brown the egg whites. Douse with rum, place on a decorative plate, and set on fire. When flames subside, spoon onto individual plates to serve.

## About This Recipe

Many versions of Baked Alaska exist. Purportedly, a chef at Delmonico's Restaurant, New York devised the original recipe to celebrate Alaskan statehood. Mitchell's father worked for Dole Pineapple in Hawaii and this family version celebrates both Hawaiian and Alaskan statehood combining the representative items of snow (meringue), pineapple, and the volcanic element of fire, common to both states. Mitchell's mother would serve this on special occasions in the early sixties. The late fifties and early sixties being the golden era of flaming desserts; tradition demanded a dimming of lights to fully appreciate the effect of the blue flames. The amount of rum would vary depending on the spirit of the occasion and rum was sometimes added to the contents after serving. Care should be taken not to set the chef's hair on fire nor that of any of the guests. The Bishop family never had a mishap with this dish but it can be perilous. Hawaiian statehood was slow in coming and followed a coup d'etat by American businessmen led by Sanford Dole in 1894. Queen Liliuokalani's overthrow led to her imprisonment in the Iolani Palace; she died in 1917. In 1898 the United States formally annexed the Hawaiian Islands. Later, in the 1970s, a revival of Hawaiian nationalism gave birth to the current separatist movement which seeks to reestablish Hawaii as a sovereign nation. Born in Hawaii before statehood, Mitchell was unaware of the dessert's political nature; instead it was fully enjoyed for its tasty and theatrical merits.

## Getty Memories

May 14, 1979 My first day at work at the Villa consisted of setting up tables and chairs around the monkey fountain at the Ranch House, eating lunch and then taking down the tables and chairs. David Bull, the Head of Paintings Conservation at the time, was hosting a lunch for visiting paintings conservators. At the time, the preparators moved office furniture as well as art and did things like set up rented tables and chairs for lunches as well as moving the art around as needed.  Jan Bull prepared the lunch in true cordon bleu style and we were invited to join. A poached salmon was involved that I remember to this day and a truly delightful selection of wines. It was a beautiful day at the Villa, one of those days in May that foreshadows summer before months of beach fog. After a somewhat lengthy lunch, we took down the tables and chairs. I went home in a somewhat dazed state. That was my first day at the Getty. Toga! Toga! In 1979 we had what has subsequently been referred to as the "Toga Party" (not to be confused with the Villa closing party) to celebrate the anniversary of the eruption of Vesuvius and destruction of the Villa dei Papyri. At the time, I was a Preparator and I think my boss had the inspiration of going to Western Costume in search of suitable attire. We found an embarrassment of riches. Western Costume had supplied Hollywood for decades and I am told much of its holdings are now in a museum. Presumably they had bought or supplied the entire wardrobe for pictures such as Ben Hur and Anthony and Cleopatra and many of the great Bible era epics of the fifties and early sixties. I found a purple toga and a faux brass breastplate with lion heads holding rings in their mouths as nipples. I think most of the helmets were modified football helmets but I think I found something in bronze painted sheet metal. Sandals of rotting leather, and a slightly moth eaten purple cloak completed the outfit. I must have looked like one of the later Generals from Gaul or Germania and as the evening progressed, the costume began to smell like it had belonged to one of them. In the interests of authenticity I did not wear my glasses for most of the evening so, sadly, the evening was literally a blur. The food however, was truly amazing. The volunteers and docents researched and prepared ancient Roman recipes or plausible alternatives, which turned out to be surprisingly good. Fresh handmade stuffed grape leaves played a prominent role. We lied around the inner peristyle on cushions in Roman fashion and a great deal of wine was consumed. I remember several rather regrettable displays and we were called upon at one point to perform a sort of mock police action since we were in military attire. Several people chose to confide their darkest secrets and hidden desires to me in the course of the evening, perhaps inspired by my temporary blindness. Of course, I cannot remember any of it. It was a truly Bacchanalian event.
