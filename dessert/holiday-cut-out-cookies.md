# Holiday Cut-Out Cookies

**Contributor:** Patricia Harpring

**Getty Entity:** Research Institute

**Department:** Vocabulary Program

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Yields 3 dozen 2-4 inch cookies Cookies: 3/4 cup white sugar 4 cups all-purpose flour 1 tablespoon baking powder 3/4 teaspoon salt 3/4 cup unsalted butter 3 large extra large eggs 1 tablespoon almond extract Icing: 2 cups confectioners' sugar 1/2 teaspoon butter 1 tablespoon almond extract food coloring

---

## Instructions

Allow the butter to reach room temperature. Cream together butter and sugar. Blend in almond extract and eggs. In a separate bowl, sift together 3 3/4 cups of the flour together with baking powder and salt. Gradually blend in the sifted flour mixture. First use an electric mixer, and when the dough becomes too stiff, use a sturdy wooden spoon. Use the extra 1/4 cup flour to firm up the dough if necessary. The dough is the proper firmness when it holds its shape well when picked up and pinched in your hand (like the consistency of Play-Doh). Chill the dough for around 3 hours. If you are in a hurry, put it in the freezer for around 30 minutes. Preheat the oven to 375Â° F. Sprinkle flour on a smooth, clean surface; you can cover the surface with waxed paper first, if you wish. Roll out around 1/3 of the dough at a time. Use a rolling pin sprinkled with flour, roll out the dough on the surface; aim for a regular shape (rectangle or circle are good shapes) until it is slightly thinner than the thickness of a piecrust, 1/8 inch thick or slightly less. Use cookie cutters to cut out various shapes. Shapes that are around 3 inches across, and that are around the same height as width are best. Tall, skinny cookie cutters create cookies that burn and break easily. Christmas trees, wreath-shapes, five- or six-pointed stars, angels, animals, and hearts are good shapes for this dough. Place the cut-out cookies on a greased cookie sheet and bake for around 7 to 10 minutes. The timing is very important -- one minute too long, and the cookies will taste burned. Experiment with the first batch to get the timing right -- the optimal baking time depends on the accuracy of your oven thermometer and the thickness and shapes of your cut-outs. The cookies should be baked through, but still white in color. The bottoms should not be brown -- just very slightly golden. Cool cookies on a surface covered with paper towels. To make icing, use 2 cups confectioners' sugar, 1/2 teaspoon butter, 1 tablespoon almond extract, the food coloring of your choice, and mix with the electric mixer while adding a few teaspoons of milk until the consistency is right (smooth and creamy, looser than icing for a cake, but not too runny to handle; if it flows off the side of the cookie when you apply it, add more confectioners' sugar). If you are making different shapes that require different colors of icing, add food coloring last, after dividing up portions of the icing into separate bowls or cups. Decorate cookies with icing and other decorations. Christmas trees and wreaths are good with green icing, adorned with cinnamon dot candies or round sprinkles. Stars are good with yellow icing and yellow-colored sugar or sprinkles. Six-pointed stars can be decorated with blue icing and blue-colored sugar. Hearts are good with pink icing and red-colored sugar. Angels and animals can be decorated with multiple colors of icing, using a pastry tube to add clothes and facial features (and whiskers and collars to the animal shapes, e.g., a cat). If your icing has very little butter in it, it should dry in an hour or two, and you can then stack the cookies in tins.

Almond-flavored cut-out cookies have been a holiday tradition in her family for generations. She learned the recipe from her German grandmother and mother. She makes them every year at Christmas/Hanukah time, and puts them in tins with other cookies and treats for her family and friends at work. She occasionally makes variations for other holidays: hearts for Valentine's Day, shamrocks for St. Patrick's day, and scary cats (iced with chocolate) for Halloween. These cookies are festive and fun to make, and decorating the cookies is a good project for children. These cookies are great for any holiday for which you can find appropriately shaped cookie-cutters. The almond extract provides the traditional flavoring for German-inspired Christmas cookies, but you may substitute vanilla extract or a little anise extract for other holidays.
