# Viennese Mid-Summer's Cake

**Contributor:** Barbara Furbush

**Getty Entity:** Museum

**Department:** Education

---

## Servings & Ingredients

Serves 12 1 angelfood cake 2-3 peaches 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg 1 teaspoon blueberries 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon 2-3 kiwi fruits 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger 1 teaspoon raspberries 1/2 teaspoon cocoa powder sprigs of mint or flowers Optional drizzle: 1 lemon 1/2 - 1 cup confectioners' sugar

---

## Instructions

Cut the cake into four thin layers. Prepare the fruits by slicing the larger fruits. For berries, mash 1/3 and gently mix with whole berries. Place the first layer of cake on the serving platter, spread the first fruit on top, and sprinkle the first spice over it. Place the second layer of cake on top and add the next fruit and spice. Continue the same process for each layer. If using the optional drizzle, sift 1/4 c. confectioners' sugar into lemon juice and stir until smooth. Gradually add more sugar until consistency is like a thin syrup. When ready to present, drizzle over top and side of cake. Garnish with mint sprigs or flowers.

## About This Recipe

During her nomadic years in the early 70s, Barbara had the luxury of time to experiment with cooking and learned culinary specialties from a variety of people all across the country. Once she discovers the basic taste principles of a culture, she intuitively begins to combine ingredients at hand - a recipe is only a beginning point. Barbara approaches her artwork in a similar fashion, initiating a matrix for a concept and then inviting others to participate in the development of the work. Variations of this cake have graced a number of celebrations in the Museum Education Department. You can substitute your own favorites for any or all ingredients. The original recipe called for yellow cake. For a rich version, use chocolate cake. Use only fresh fruits, not dried, canned, or frozen. The hidden beauty of the cake is that won't destroy your diet! Use long toothpicks to hold it together if you need to transport it.
